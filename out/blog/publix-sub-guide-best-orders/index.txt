The Pub Sub isn't just a sandwich — it's a regional obsession that separates the transplants from the natives faster than asking someone where they went to high school.</p>
<p>After 23 years of showing people around Tampa Bay, I've watched countless newcomers discover the Pub Sub phenomenon. Some get it immediately. Others need gentle guidance. Consider this your comprehensive field guide to understanding, ordering, and appreciating what might be Florida's greatest culinary contribution to the world.</p>
<h2>What Exactly Is a Pub Sub?</h2>
<p>Let's start with the basics. A Pub Sub is a submarine sandwich made at the deli counter of any Publix supermarket. But calling it "just a sandwich" is like calling the Gulf of Mexico "just some water." These aren't your typical grocery store deli offerings — Publix has turned sandwich-making into an art form that rivals dedicated sub shops.</p>
<p>The magic happens at the deli counter, where they use boar's head meats, fresh vegetables, and house-made sauces on crusty sub rolls that somehow manage to hold everything together without falling apart. The bread alone — baked fresh daily in each store — would make some dedicated bakeries jealous.</p>
<p>What makes Pub Subs special isn't just the ingredients. It's the system. Every Publix employee knows the drill, the ordering language is universal across hundreds of locations, and the quality stays remarkably consistent whether you're in downtown Tampa or rural Pasco County.</p>
<h2>The Essential Pub Sub Vocabulary</h2>
<p>Before you step up to any deli counter in Tampa Bay, you need to speak the language. Here's your crash course:</p>
<p><strong>The Basics:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>"Whole" or "half" — referring to the size</li>
<li>"Tossed" — means they mix everything together</li>
<li>"On the side" — condiments in separate containers</li>
<li>"Toasted" or "pressed" — how you want the bread treated</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The Proteins:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>"Chicken tender sub" — the holy grail, made with their hot fried chicken tenders</li>
<li>"Turkey and ham" — classic combination, usually with provolone</li>
<li>"Italian" — salami, ham, pepperoni, provolone</li>
<li>"Roast beef" — their premium deli roast beef</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The Add-ons:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>"Everything on it" — lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles, oil, vinegar, salt, pepper</li>
<li>"LTO" — just lettuce, tomato, onion</li>
<li>"Spicy mustard" — their house mustard with a kick</li>
<li>"Pub sauce" — their signature sandwich sauce</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Chicken Tender Sub: Tampa Bay's Crown Jewel</h2>
<p>If you order exactly one Pub Sub in your Tampa Bay lifetime, make it the chicken tender sub. This isn't hyperbole — it's practically a religious experience disguised as lunch.</p>
<p>The genius lies in using their hot, fresh chicken tenders straight from the deli fryer. These aren't reheated frozen strips. They're hand-breaded, seasoned chicken tenders that would hold their own at any decent restaurant. The standard order: whole chicken tender sub with mayo, lettuce, tomato, and cheese, toasted.</p>
<p>But here's where it gets interesting. The customization options are endless:</p>
<p><strong>The Classic:</strong> Mayo, lettuce, tomato, provolone, toasted
<strong>The Buffalo:</strong> Tossed in buffalo sauce with ranch, lettuce, tomato
<strong>The Chipotle:</strong> Chipotle sauce, pepper jack, lettuce, tomato
<strong>The Loaded:</strong> Mayo, mustard, lettuce, tomato, pickles, onions, oil and vinegar</p>
<p>Pro tip from someone who's ordered hundreds of these: ask for it "pressed" after they add the tenders. This melds everything together and creates the perfect texture contrast between the crispy coating and soft bread.</p>
<h2>Where to Find the Best Pub Subs in Tampa Bay</h2>
<p>While every Publix makes decent Pub Subs, some locations have earned legendary status among locals. Here's where the magic really happens:</p>
<h3>Publix at Hyde Park Village (1602 W Snow Ave, Tampa)</h3>
<p>This location serves the Bayshore crowd and has mastered the art of the quick, perfect sub. The deli staff here moves with military precision during lunch rush, but never sacrifices quality. They're also one of the few locations that consistently stocks their specialty breads.</p>
<h3>Publix at International Plaza (4080 W Boy Scout Blvd, Tampa)</h3>
<p>Popular with airport workers and travelers, this location has perfected the grab-and-go Pub Sub. They understand urgency without cutting corners, and their chicken tenders seem perpetually fresh due to high turnover.</p>
<h3>Publix on Dale Mabry (3808 W Northgate Blvd, Tampa)</h3>
<p>A neighborhood favorite that treats Pub Sub making like performance art. The deli counter staff here genuinely cares about getting your order exactly right, and they're not afraid to make suggestions based on what looks best that day.</p>
<h3>Publix at Wiregrass Commons (28212 Paseo Dr, Wesley Chapel)</h3>
<p>Serving the rapidly growing Wesley Chapel area, this newer location has embraced the Pub Sub culture with enthusiasm. Their produce is consistently fresh, and they stock a wider variety of specialty cheeses than many locations.</p>
<h3>Publix on Nebraska Avenue (1725 W Hillsborough Ave, Tampa)</h3>
<p>This might be the most authentic Pub Sub experience in Tampa Bay. It's where locals go, not tourists. The staff has been there forever, knows regular customers by name, and can make your usual order without being asked.</p>
<h2>Beyond Chicken Tenders: Other Essential Orders</h2>
<p>While the chicken tender sub gets most of the attention, limiting yourself to just one type of Pub Sub is like only visiting Clearwater Beach when Tampa Bay has dozens of incredible coastal spots.</p>
<h3>The Ultimate Italian</h3>
<p>This is Publix's answer to the classic Italian hero. Genoa salami, ham, pepperoni, capicola, and provolone with lettuce, tomato, onion, oil, and vinegar. Ask for it on their Italian bread if available, and request that they drizzle the oil and vinegar directly on the meat before adding vegetables.</p>
<h3>The Turkey and Havarti Club</h3>
<p>Sliced turkey breast, havarti cheese, bacon, lettuce, tomato, and mayo on toasted bread. This sounds simple but showcases the quality of their ingredients. The turkey is actually carved from whole roasted birds, not processed lunch meat.</p>
<h3>The Veggie Deluxe</h3>
<p>Don't sleep on Publix's vegetarian options. Their veggie sub with multiple cheeses, avocado, sprouts, cucumbers, and their herb oil is legitimately satisfying. Many locations now offer hummus as a spread option, which transforms the entire sandwich.</p>
<h3>The Seafood Special</h3>
<p>When it's available, the lobster salad sub is a splurge worth making. It's not cheap — expect to pay $18-20 for a whole sub — but it's real lobster salad, not imitation. The crab cake sub, when available, is similarly impressive.</p>
<h2>The Art of Ordering Like a Local</h2>
<p>There's a rhythm to ordering Pub Subs that separates the newcomers from the established locals. Here's how to blend in:</p>
<p><strong>Approach with confidence.</strong> Don't study the menu board for five minutes. Know what you want before you get to the counter.</p>
<p><strong>Use the shorthand.</strong> "Whole chicken tender, mayo, LTO, provolone, toasted" is much more efficient than describing each ingredient in detail.</p>
<p><strong>Be specific about quantities.</strong> If you want extra pickles, say "extra pickles." If you want light mayo, say "light mayo." These folks make hundreds of subs daily — precision helps everyone.</p>
<p><strong>Know the timing.</strong> Lunch rush (11:30 AM to 1:30 PM) means longer waits but fresher ingredients. Late afternoon means shorter lines but potentially picked-over selections.</p>
<p><strong>Ask about specials.</strong> Many locations rotate specialty sandwiches or have ingredients that aren't on the standard menu. The staff is usually happy to share what's available.</p>
<h2>Pub Sub Hacks That Actually Work</h2>
<p>After two decades of Tampa Bay living, I've picked up some tricks that make the Pub Sub experience even better:</p>
<h3>The Split Decision</h3>
<p>Order a whole sub but ask them to cut it into three pieces instead of two. This creates perfect snack portions and makes it easier to share.</p>
<h3>The Toasting Trick</h3>
<p>Ask for your sub to be "lightly toasted" rather than regular toasting. This warms everything through without making the bread too crunchy to bite comfortably.</p>
<h3>The Sauce Strategy</h3>
<p>Request sauce on the side for any sub you're not eating immediately. This prevents soggy bread and lets you control the flavor intensity.</p>
<h3>The Meat Mix</h3>
<p>Most locations will let you combine proteins for a small upcharge. Half turkey, half ham creates interesting flavor combinations without committing to one protein.</p>
<h3>The Produce Upgrade</h3>
<p>Ask what vegetables look best that day. Sometimes they have specialty items like roasted red peppers or fresh mozzarella that aren't prominently displayed.</p>
<h2>When Pub Subs Go Wrong (And How to Avoid It)</h2>
<p>Not every Pub Sub experience is magical. Here are the common pitfalls and how to navigate them:</p>
<h3>The Soggy Syndrome</h3>
<p>Too much dressing or wet vegetables can turn your perfect sub into a mushy disappointment. Combat this by asking for dressings on the side and avoiding tomatoes if your sub will sit for more than an hour.</p>
<h3>The Proportion Problem</h3>
<p>Some counter workers are heavy-handed with vegetables and light on protein. Don't be afraid to ask for "extra meat" or "light on the lettuce" to get the balance right.</p>
<h3>The Temperature Trap</h3>
<p>Cold subs that should be warm (like chicken tender subs) are devastating. Always specify "hot chicken tenders" to ensure you're getting fresh, warm protein.</p>
<h2>The Economics of Pub Sub Addiction</h2>
<p>Let's talk money. A whole chicken tender sub runs about $8-10 depending on add-ons, which puts it in the same price range as fast-casual chains but with significantly better quality. When you consider that a whole sub easily provides two meals for most people, the value proposition becomes even stronger.</p>
<p>Compare this to similar options in Tampa Bay:</p>
<ul>
<li>Jersey Mike's whole sub: $12-15</li>
<li>Jimmy John's comparable sandwich: $10-13</li>
<li>Local deli equivalent: $11-16</li>
</ul>
<p>The Publix advantage isn't just price — it's convenience. With over 200 locations in the Tampa Bay area, you're never more than a few minutes from a Pub Sub. Try finding a Jersey Mike's in rural Hernando County.</p>
<h2>Pub Subs and Tampa Bay Culture</h2>
<p>Understanding Pub Subs means understanding something fundamental about Tampa Bay culture. This region values authenticity over pretension, quality over marketing, and accessibility over exclusivity. The Pub Sub embodies all of these values.</p>
<p>It's food that works for construction workers grabbing lunch in Plant City and executives meeting clients in downtown Tampa. It's equally at home at a Little League game in <a href="/cities/clearwater/">Clearwater</a> or a beach picnic in St. Pete. This democratic appeal reflects Tampa Bay's unpretentious, practical character.</p>
<p>The Pub Sub also represents Florida's genius for taking something ordinary and making it extraordinary through attention to detail and understanding of local preferences. It's the same instinct that turned swampland into beautiful communities and fishing villages into world-class destinations.</p>

<h2>Regional Variations Worth Knowing</h2>
<p>While Pub Subs maintain consistency across locations, subtle regional preferences have emerged throughout Tampa Bay:</p>
<p><strong>Pinellas County</strong> locations tend to offer more seafood options, reflecting the coastal culture. The grouper sandwich, when available, is consistently better at beaches-area Publix stores.</p>
<p><strong>Hillsborough County</strong> locations, especially around <a href="/cities/tampa/">Tampa</a>, often have the widest selection of specialty cheeses and gourmet add-ons, catering to the urban palate.</p>
<p><strong>Pasco County</strong> stores focus on hearty, straightforward combinations that appeal to the area's working-class roots. Their portion sizes often run slightly larger.</p>
<p><strong>Hernando County</strong> locations pride themselves on personalized service. The staff often remembers regular customers and their usual orders.</p>
<h2>The Pub Sub as Social Currency</h2>
<p>In Tampa Bay, knowing how to order a proper Pub Sub functions as a form of social currency. It signals that you understand local customs and have moved beyond tourist status. Business lunches conducted over Pub Subs carry a different energy than those held at formal restaurants — they're more relaxed, more genuine, more focused on actual conversation.</p>
<p>I've closed real estate deals over chicken tender subs, celebrated successful relocations with Italian subs, and comforted homesick transplants with perfectly crafted turkey clubs. The Pub Sub creates common ground in a region where people arrive from everywhere else.</p>
<h2>Seasonal Considerations and Limited Offerings</h2>
<p>Publix occasionally rolls out limited-time sandwich offerings that become legendary among locals. The Cuban sandwich, available at select locations during certain periods, rivals anything you'll find in Ybor City. Their occasional prime rib sandwich uses actual slow-roasted beef and costs accordingly.</p>
<p>Holiday seasons bring specialty items: turkey subs using their premium Thanksgiving turkeys, ham subs featuring their spiral-cut holiday hams. These limited offerings often represent the best value in the entire store.</p>
<p>Summer months mean peak Pub Sub season. Beach trips, boat outings, pool parties, and outdoor events all demand portable, delicious food that won't spoil in Florida heat. The Pub Sub handles these challenges better than most alternatives.</p>
<h2>The Future of Pub Subs in Tampa Bay</h2>
<p>As Tampa Bay continues growing and changing, the Pub Sub culture evolves too. Newer Publix locations feature expanded deli sections, more specialty ingredients, and sometimes even seating areas for immediate consumption. Some stores now offer online ordering, though purists argue this diminishes the interactive experience of working with the deli staff.</p>
<p>The challenge will be maintaining quality and character as the region adds hundreds of thousands of new residents over the next decade. The Pub Sub's democratic appeal — equally satisfying to longtime locals and curious newcomers — suggests it will adapt and survive.</p>
<p>Recent menu additions like plant-based proteins and gluten-free bread options show Publix responding to changing dietary preferences without abandoning their core identity. This balance between tradition and innovation mirrors Tampa Bay's own evolution.</p>
<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>What's the most popular Pub Sub order in Tampa Bay?</h3>
<p>The chicken tender sub with mayo, lettuce, tomato, and cheese remains the overwhelming favorite. Most locals order it toasted on white or wheat bread. This combination showcases Publix's fresh chicken tenders while keeping the flavors simple and satisfying.</p>
<h3>How much should I expect to pay for a Pub Sub?</h3>
<p>Whole subs range from $7-12 depending on ingredients, with chicken tender subs typically costing $8-10. Half subs run about 60% of the whole sub price. Premium options like seafood or prime rib can reach $15-20, but standard combinations stay affordable.</p>
<h3>Can I order Pub Subs online or do I need to visit the store?</h3>
<p>Most Tampa Bay Publix locations now offer online ordering through their app or website, with options for pickup or delivery. However, many locals prefer ordering in person to customize their sandwich and interact with the deli staff for the full experience.</p>
<h3>What's the difference between getting it "toasted" versus "pressed"?</h3>
<p>Toasted means the assembled sandwich goes into a convection oven to warm the bread and melt cheese. Pressed means it goes into a panini press that compresses the sandwich while heating it, creating a different texture and helping flavors meld together.</p>
<h3>Are there any secret menu items or special orders I should know about?</h3>
<p>While there's no official secret menu, experienced deli staff can often accommodate special requests like mixing different meats, adding specialty cheeses not on display, or using different bread types. The key is asking politely and being flexible based on what's available that day.</p>
<h3>How long do Pub Subs stay fresh, and what's the best way to store them?</h3>
<p>Pub Subs are best eaten within 4-6 hours of purchase, especially those with fresh vegetables. If storing longer, ask for dressings on the side and refrigerate immediately. Avoid freezing, as the bread texture deteriorates significantly when thawed.</p>
